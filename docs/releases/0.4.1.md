# OpenWatch 0.4.1

Released 2026-11-22.

OpenWatch now shows, in the app, every kind of health data it reads and writes, which device it comes from and why, and asks for two permissions fewer. A Garmin watch keeps syncing when one data type is not allowed, and its workouts are no longer lost while sync is paused. The watch's notifications respect what Android hides on the lock screen. The scale's key and the watches' link tokens are kept encrypted and stay behind when you move to a new phone. Underneath, the devices now live in the app's database, so a second watch of the same kind keeps records of its own.

### Added

- **What OpenWatch reads and writes.** A screen lists every Health Connect data type the app writes, under the device it comes from (a Garmin watch, a Wear OS watch, the scale), every type it reads and what for, and what the watch app asks for on the watch, with the switch each one sits behind. It opens from Settings, from the Health Connect setup, and from Health Connect's own permission page, which until now spoke of seven types. Skin temperature, blood oxygen and breathing rate from a Wear OS watch are named there, as they are in the privacy policy.
- **Choose the apps that send notifications to the watch.** With "Only apps I choose" on, only the apps switched on send; off, as before, every app sends but the ones switched off. Each list is kept when you switch between them.
- **Hide sensitive content.** On by default: where Android's settings hide an app's sensitive content on the lock screen, the watch gets the notification's title only. A notification marked secret, or from a channel set not to show on the lock screen, never reaches the watch.
- **Connected apps names the app.** Each app's card shows its package name and the key it is signed with, and warns when an app takes the name of another app it is not.
- **A running workout on the watch face.** While a workout runs, the watch shows its sport and a stopwatch, or Paused, as an ongoing activity; a tap opens the workout.

### Changed

- **Two permissions fewer.** OpenWatch no longer asks to read steps or to write wheelchair pushes, which nothing used, and each permission is declared once.
- **A sync that fails says why.** The watch screen names the cause in your language: a missing permission, Bluetooth off or not allowed, sync paused, the device out of reach, Health Connect asking OpenWatch to slow down, or records Health Connect would not take. A missing permission or a paused sync now waits for you instead of being retried.
- **Several devices sync at once.** A long Garmin sync no longer holds back the other devices, and the live heart rate and steps show on the watch they came from.
- **A scheduled sync finishes its work.** It runs for up to eight minutes and then continues in up to three follow-up runs, instead of being stopped by Android partway through. A sync you start, or one the watch announces, keeps the phone awake until it ends, for thirty minutes at most.
- **Garmin records say a Garmin watch recorded them.** Sleep, heart rate, steps and the other wellness records are now written as recorded automatically by a Garmin watch, not as entered by hand on a phone. Records written before keep their label until they are written again.
- **Secrets stay on this phone.** The scale's key and the Wear OS link tokens are kept encrypted under a key held by the Android Keystore, on the phone and on the watch. When Android moves the app to a new phone, the scale's key, the link tokens and the list of apps you allowed stay behind, so the new phone asks for them again; the sync progress moves, so it does not fetch everything again.
- **A second watch of the same kind.** Each paired device has an entry of its own in the app's database. The first Garmin and the first Wear OS watch keep their records as they are; a second one of the same kind writes records of its own instead of overwriting the first's.
- **The scale is a paired device like the watches.** Apps connected over OpenLink see it under a random id instead of its Bluetooth address.
- **Breathing rate on more nights.** One clear reading of a window now counts when the second method finds none, which on a Galaxy Watch7 had left most nights without a value.
- **Languages.** This release's new screens and messages are translated into all thirteen languages of the phone app.
- **Logs.** A release build of the watch app logs no reading, and the phone app logs no Bluetooth address, app name or notification action.

### Fixed

- **A Garmin watch stopped syncing for one missing permission.** When one data type was not allowed, the whole import failed and every later sync stopped at it, while the watch had already archived its files. Now only that type is left out, and the watch screen names it with a button to allow it.
- **A large first sync failed in Health Connect.** Records now go in requests of at most a thousand.
- **Workouts while sync was paused.** A Garmin workout was still written with sync paused, and one that did not reach Health Connect was lost. It now waits on the phone and is imported by the next sync.
- **One unreadable device forgot them all.** A damaged entry in the device list no longer clears every paired device, and removing a device forgets everything it left behind.
- **A Garmin watch's longer answers.** The phone sent replies over 375 bytes whole, which a vívoactive 5 refused, asking for them again every minute; they now go in parts.
- **Fixes:** a watch that is not a Garmin is no longer synced as one; two syncs of one device can no longer start at once; the watch keeps its link to the phone when Android refuses part of its foreground start; Allow on the watch answers the phone whose request you read; a deleted weigh-in no longer comes back from an older broadcast; a body profile from another app stamped in the future can no longer block your changes; a step minute already in Health Connect is not written again; pairing failures and a failed "find my watch" are shown, in your language; CoMaps guidance comes only from CoMaps itself.

The phone app and the Wear OS app share the package name `tech.mmarca.openwatch` and the signing certificate, so both install as a normal update; the watch's versionCode is 2000000000 plus the phone's.

Distribution flow:

- GitHub release with the signed phone APK, signed debug APK, signed Wear OS APK and both signed Android App Bundle assets.
- Direct Google Play production upload, to the phone track and the Wear OS track, from the approved production run of the release workflow.
